# Electrons in a Magnetic Field, Quantum Hall Effect and Landau Levels {#session-4}

In the last session we built the general tools of topological band structure. These were the Kubo formula for the Hall conductivity, the way to write it as an integral of the Berry curvature, the Chern number as the topological invariant that comes out of it, and the bulk-boundary correspondence, which says that a nonzero Chern number gives a protected chiral edge state. We applied these tools to two concrete examples, the SSH chain and a gapped Dirac cone on the honeycomb lattice, which is the Haldane construction, and we did this on purpose without any magnetic field. This choice left one case open. In session 2 we saw that the dispersion of a Bloch Hamiltonian comes in three basic types, parabolic, Dirac and flat, and the flat-band case, which is the one where a real magnetic field gives its own topological story, was left for this session. So let us continue from the point where we stopped. In this session we apply the Chern-number tools of session 3 to the flat-band case in full. First we will see the integer quantum Hall effect and Landau levels as topological flat bands. Then we will let these flat bands become real many-body objects, and we will see the Laughlin wavefunction, fractional charge and anyonic statistics, which are the simplest example of fractionalization that we will meet in this course. At the end we will go back to the case without a field. The Haldane model gives a quantized Hall conductance with no magnetic field at all, and we will see explicitly that it and the ordinary quantum Hall state are two forms of the same topological phase.
