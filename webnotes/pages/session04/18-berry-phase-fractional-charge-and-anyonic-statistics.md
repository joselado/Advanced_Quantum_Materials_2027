## Berry phase, fractional charge, and anyonic statistics {#sec-4-18 number="4.18"}

We now use the Berry phase, which is the same object that we used in session 3 to build the Chern number, to show directly two things. First, the quasihole excitation of Section [4.17](#sec-4-17) has a fraction of the electron charge. Second, a pair of them follow neither fermionic nor bosonic exchange statistics.

**Fractional charge.** We take the wavefunction with one quasihole [(4.14)](#eq-4-14) and move $w$ adiabatically around a closed loop. We write $w=|w|e^{i\theta}$ and compute the Berry phase that we obtain when $\theta$ goes from $0$ to $2\pi$. The result is an Aharonov-Bohm phase, proportional to the flux enclosed by the loop, but with an extra prefactor $1/m$ with respect to the phase that an ordinary particle with charge $e$ would get around the same loop. The Aharonov-Bohm phase for a particle with charge $q$ around an enclosed flux $\Phi$ is $2\pi
q\Phi/\Phi_0$, where $\Phi_0$ is the flux quantum, so the extra factor $1/m$ that we find is a direct and clear statement about the *charge* of the excitation,

$$q_{\text{qh}} = \frac{e}{m}. \tag{4.15}$$

For these excitations the electron is no longer the basic unit of charge, and the unit is a real fraction of it.

**Anyonic statistics.** Now we take a wavefunction with *two* quasiholes, one fixed at the origin and one at $w$, and we move the second one in a loop around the first, again with $w=|w|e^{i\theta}$. The Berry phase that we obtain has two parts. The first part is the same Aharonov-Bohm contribution as before, given by the charge times the enclosed flux. The second part is an additional **statistical phase**, which comes only from the fact that one quasiparticle goes around the other,

$$\theta_{\text{stat}} = \frac{2\pi}{m}. \tag{4.16}$$

To compare with the familiar cases, note that to move one quasiparticle all the way around another is *two* exchanges, one after the other, so the exchange phase is half of [(4.16)](#eq-4-16), which is $\pi/m$. For ordinary fermions the exchange of two particles gives a phase $\pi$, and for bosons it gives a phase $0$. The exchange phase $\pi/m$ is neither of the two, and it goes from one familiar case to the other as $m$ grows, as shown in figure [4.6](#fig:s04:quasihole). These quasiparticles are what are called **anyons**, which are excitations with exchange statistics that go continuously from the fermionic to the bosonic case, and they are labeled by the same integer $m$ that fixed the filling fraction. We started from a many-body wavefunction built only from ordinary electrons, which are real fermions, and from the correlations of this wavefunction we obtained excitations that are neither fermions nor bosons. This is the simplest example of fractionalization in a quantum many-body system that we will meet in this course.
