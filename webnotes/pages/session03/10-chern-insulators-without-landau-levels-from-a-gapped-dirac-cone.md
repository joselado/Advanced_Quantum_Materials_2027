## Chern insulators without Landau levels from a gapped Dirac cone {#sec-3-10 number="3.10"}

Now we come back to the question from the beginning of the session. A Chern insulator needs a broken time-reversal symmetry. In fact, one can show directly from [(3.4)](#eq-3-4) that $\sigma_{xy}\equiv 0$ whenever time-reversal symmetry holds, since time reversal changes the sign of the Berry curvature. However, nothing in the derivation of the Chern number required a magnetic field or Landau levels. So the question is if we can obtain a finite Chern number from an ordinary lattice band structure, using only terms that break time-reversal symmetry and that are entirely inside the lattice itself.

The method is to look for a lattice that has a **massive Dirac equation** in its low-energy expansion. Several two-dimensional lattices have massless Dirac points in their band structure, such as the honeycomb lattice, the kagome lattice and a square lattice with $\pi$-flux, and this is the same Dirac cone that we derived for graphene in session 2. Close to one such point, the low-energy Hamiltonian has the form

$$H(k) = k_x\,\sigma_x + \lambda\, k_y\,\sigma_y + m\,\sigma_z, \tag{3.7}$$

with $\lambda = \pm 1$ fixed by the chirality of the lattice at this Dirac point, and $m$ a mass term that opens a gap, $E_\pm(k) = \pm\sqrt{k_x^2+k_y^2+m^2}$. We compute the Berry curvature of the lower band and integrate it, with the same procedure as in [(3.4)](#eq-3-4) but now over the small region of momentum space where the curvature is concentrated. This gives the Chern number of this single gapped Dirac cone,

$$C_{\text{Dirac}} = \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{sgn}(m)\,\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda). \tag{3.8}$$

The half-integer in [(3.8)](#eq-3-8) is not an inconsistency. A real lattice always has an even number of Dirac points related by time-reversal symmetry, and the honeycomb lattice has two, which are usually called $K$ and $K'$. Only the *sum* of their contributions needs to be an integer.

This is where the choice of the mass term matters, and we will work out the two natural choices explicitly, because they give qualitatively different physics. At $K$ and $K'$ the chirality $\lambda$ has opposite sign, since the two points are time-reversal partners. Let us first open the gap with a staggered sublattice potential, which raises the onsite energy on one honeycomb sublattice and lowers it on the other. This mass term is an ordinary scalar potential, so it has the *same* sign $m$ at both $K$ and $K'$. From [(3.8)](#eq-3-8), the two Dirac points then give Chern numbers of opposite sign, $+\tfrac12\,\mathrm{sgn}(m)$ and $-\tfrac12\,\mathrm{sgn}(m)$, which cancel. The result is $C=0$, which is an ordinary insulator that is topologically trivial, and this is the same kind of gap as in boron nitride. Now let us instead open the gap with a purely imaginary second-neighbor hopping, which is called the **Haldane coupling**. Physically, this term is a pattern of local orbital currents that circulate inside each hexagon, and it is odd under time reversal. This mass term changes sign between $K$ and $K'$ *relative to* $\lambda$, so the two Dirac points now give Chern numbers of the *same* sign, which add and do not cancel. The result is $C = \pm 1$, a real Chern insulator, built only from lattice hoppings and without any external magnetic field. This was the first model ever proposed with a finite and quantized Hall conductivity and zero net flux, and it is the mechanism to open a gap in the Dirac cone of session 2 that we were looking for. Figure [3.5](#fig:s03:masses) shows the two cases side by side, each on the lattice and in the spectrum of a ribbon, where only the Haldane case has edge states that cross the gap. A gap can also be opened by combining an ordinary magnetization with spin-orbit coupling, which gives the same sign pattern as the Haldane term and an equivalent quantum anomalous Hall state. Both routes have material realizations. Physics similar to the Haldane model has been realized in twisted graphene bilayers on boron nitride, and the route with magnetism and spin-orbit coupling has been realized and measured in Cr-doped (Bi,Sb)$_2$Te$_3$.
