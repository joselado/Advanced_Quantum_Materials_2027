## Closing summary {#sec-8-14 number="8.14"}

This is the final recap of the lecture, with the equations that it refers to.

- Magnetism, in all the forms that we saw in Sessions 1 and 6 and in this session, comes from strong repulsive interactions between electrons.

- Frustrated magnetic models, such as those on triangular and kagome lattices (Section [8.4](#sec-8-4)), can show **quantum spin liquid** behavior. This is a maximally entangled ground state with zero local spin expectation value on every site [(8.5)](#eq-8-5). It generalizes the singlet of the Heisenberg dimer [(8.3)](#eq-8-3), through the valence bond solid state [(8.5)](#eq-8-5), to a resonating and frustrated ground state [(8.6)](#eq-8-6)-[(8.7)](#eq-8-7), and conventional mean-field theory cannot describe it by construction.

- The zero magnetization of a QSL is the opposite regime from the one that Holstein-Primakoff needs [(8.9)](#eq-8-9). For this reason, its fundamental excitations are not magnons but **spinons**, with $S=1/2$. These are emergent fermions without charge that we obtain from the Abrikosov or parton construction, [(8.10)](#eq-8-10)-[(8.15)](#eq-8-15), which is the mean-field method for electrons of Sessions 1 and 6 applied to the spin sector. Spinon bands can be gapless, gapped, or of Dirac type (Section [8.9](#sec-8-9)).

- The parton construction has an emergent gauge redundancy [(8.16)](#eq-8-16). The mean-field bond variable is a link field, and its topological defects are gauge excitations called **visons**, which are in general coupled to the spinons (Section [8.10](#sec-8-10)).

- Quantum entanglement that mixes spin and fermion degrees of freedom also appears in **Kondo systems**. The same parton method, applied to a lattice of local moments inside a metal, [(8.17)](#eq-8-17)-[(8.21)](#eq-8-21), turns the Kondo exchange coupling into a mean-field **hybridization** $\gamma_K$. This hybridization opens a gap in the conduction band at the anti-crossing and turns a metal into a **Kondo insulator**, also called a heavy-fermion insulator, and the gap grows monotonically with the strength of the Kondo coupling (Section [8.13](#sec-8-13)).

Session 9 continues directly from this construction. The method of Abrikosov fermions and spinons that we built here is used again, now for a single magnetic impurity in a metal that is otherwise ordinary, with an explicit reference to a result on Friedel oscillations that is derived earlier in that same session.
