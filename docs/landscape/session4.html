Session 4. Electrons in magnetic fields, the quantum Hall effect and Landau levels

Learning outcomes

  • Relate the quantized Hall conductivity to the Chern number of the filled Landau levels
  • Derive the Landau levels of Schrödinger and Dirac electrons, linear in B and in √B
  • Explain why a ribbon in a magnetic field has flat bulk levels and chiral edge states
  • Construct the filled lowest Landau level in the symmetric gauge as a uniform droplet
  • Explain how the Laughlin wavefunction gives fractional charge e/m and anyonic statistics
  • Recognize the Haldane model as a quantum Hall state without Landau levels

Contents

  • Where the quantum Hall state appears
  • Hall conductivity, Chern number and quantized plateaus
  • Electrons coupled to a magnetic field, Landau levels and gauge choice
  • Landau levels of Schrödinger and Dirac electrons, and on the lattice
  • Ladder operators for Landau levels
  • Symmetric gauge, angular momentum and the filled lowest Landau level
  • The Laughlin wavefunction
  • Quasiholes, fractional charge and anyons
  • The Haldane model, and real Chern insulators

Simulations shown

  • Landau levels of the Hofstadter model on the square lattice, each with Chern number 1
  • Square-lattice ribbon in a magnetic field, flat Landau levels bending into chiral edge states
  • Hall resistivity against field at fixed density, with plateaus at h/(νe²)
  • Landau fan against field, levels linear in B for Schrödinger and in √B for Dirac electrons
  • Armchair graphene ribbon in a magnetic field, the Dirac Landau levels and their edge states
  • Density of the filled lowest Landau level and of the Laughlin state at ν = 1/3
  • Quasihole in the Laughlin droplet with a missing charge 1/3, and the phase of winding it
  • Haldane model, its bands, Berry curvature and ribbon edge states, deformed to the quantum Hall state without closing the gap
