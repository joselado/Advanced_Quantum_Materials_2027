Session 7. Quantum entanglement and tensor networks

Learning outcomes

  • Map fermionic Hamiltonians to spin Hamiltonians with the Jordan-Wigner transformation
  • Explain why storing a many-body wave function costs 2^L numbers
  • Compute the entanglement entropy of a product state and of a singlet
  • Relate the area law of gapped ground states to the success of matrix product states
  • Recognize when matrix product states fail: higher dimensions, long time evolution, excited states
  • Explain how the density matrix renormalization group finds a ground state one tensor at a time

Contents

  • Weakly and strongly correlated matter
  • Fermionic and spin Hamiltonians, the Jordan-Wigner transformation
  • Storing the many-body wave function
  • From the Heisenberg dimer to a Hilbert space growing as 2^L
  • The matrix product state ansatz
  • Product states, entangled states and the entanglement entropy
  • Dimerized chain and dimerized square lattice, the area law
  • Matrix product states as area-law states, and where they fail
  • Tensor-network diagrams, and ground states with DMRG
  • Beyond matrix product states, MERA and PEPS

Simulations shown

  • Ground state of a 10-site Heisenberg chain by exact diagonalization, its 1024 coefficients as a map
  • Matrix product state of a 14-site Heisenberg chain, numbers stored and error against bond dimension m
  • Entanglement entropy of a two-site state going from a product state to the singlet, up to log 2
  • Block entropy of the dimerized chain, 2 log 2 regardless of the block length
  • Entropy of square partitions of a tetramer lattice, growing with the boundary
  • Half-chain entropy of a 16-site Heisenberg chain after a quench, growing linearly in time
  • DMRG sweep on a 10-site Heisenberg chain, energy converging to the exact one
  • Block entropy of the transverse-field Ising chain, growing as log ℓ at criticality, saturating when gapped
