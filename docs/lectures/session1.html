Session 1. Second quantization, mean field and spontaneous symmetry breaking

The video lecture of this session is not ready yet. The landscape lecture of this session is in the other tab.

Learning outcomes

  • Write a single-particle and a quartic Hamiltonian with creation and annihilation operators
  • Explain why the many-body problem grows as 2^L while the single-particle one grows as L
  • Recognize a symmetry-broken state and the symmetry it breaks, for magnets, ferroelectrics and superconductors
  • Explain why a magnet breaks time-reversal symmetry, and how the Hall conductivity or the Kerr effect detects it
  • Derive the mean-field decoupling of an interaction and solve it self-consistently
  • Relate the charge density wave, magnetism and superconductivity to the sign and form of the interaction

Contents

  • Quantum materials and the exotic phenomena they show
  • Emergent excitations, from atoms to materials
  • First and second quantization, the particle in a box
  • Creation and annihilation operators and fermionic statistics
  • The fermionic many-body problem and its 2^L states
  • Interactions and correlated states
  • Single-particle Hamiltonians on graphene islands
  • Spontaneous symmetry breaking and the classification of matter by broken symmetries
  • Magnets and time-reversal symmetry
  • Mean field for the charge density wave, the Hubbard model and superconductivity

Simulations shown

  • Acoustic phonon branch of a chain of masses and springs, gapless at k = 0
  • Levels and wavefunctions of a chain of 200 sites, the particle in a box with levels 1 : 4 : 9
  • Ring of five sites diagonalized in first and in second quantization, giving the same levels
  • Eight states of three interacting sites and the 2^L growth of the many-body Hilbert space
  • Electronic states of triangular, hexagonal and rectangular graphene islands, the |ψ|² that STM maps
  • Charge density wave of a spinless chain in mean field, the gap and the charge imbalance growing with V
  • Néel state of the Hubbard chain in mean field, the gap U·M approaching U at large U
  • Onsite pairing Δ of the attractive Hubbard chain, solved self-consistently
