Session 4. Electrons in a Magnetic Field, Quantum Hall Effect and Landau Levels

4.15 Angular momentum, enclosed flux, and the uniform electron gas

In Section 4.13 we saw that a larger angular momentum \(m\) moves the maximum of an orbital further from the origin, to the radius \(r_m=\sqrt{2m}\,\ell_B\). In other words, this orbital encloses a larger area, and so a larger magnetic flux. We will use this link between angular momentum and enclosed flux in Section 4.17 to find how much charge a quasiparticle has. For now, the important consequence is about (4.11) itself. When we sum over the full Slater determinant, we find a density profile that is essentially uniform, or flat, up to a radius set by the highest occupied angular momentum \(m=N-1\), and that drops sharply to zero beyond this radius. In other words, the filled lowest Landau level is a uniform two-dimensional electron gas without any structure, and this is the property that we will now ask also from a Landau level with fractional filling. Figure 4.5 shows both parts of this statement, with the single orbitals as nested rings of growing radius, and the flat droplet that they give when we add them.
