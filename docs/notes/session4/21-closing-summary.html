Session 4. Electrons in a Magnetic Field, Quantum Hall Effect and Landau Levels

4.21 Closing summary

  • The Hall conductivity is a topological invariant, the Chern number of the occupied bands, as we derived in more detail in session 3. Equation (4.1) is the same result, which we confirm here and do not derive again from the beginning.

  • A magnetic field turns a two-dimensional electron gas into a set of flat Landau levels, each with Chern number one, so the Hall conductivity is quantized to \(N e^2/h\) for \(N\) filled Landau levels, equation (4.2).

  • An insulating bulk still conducts because a nonzero Chern number gives chiral edge states without backscattering at the boundary of the sample (Section 4.5), which we can see directly in the band structures of ribbons.

  • Landau levels come from minimal coupling (4.3) or, on a lattice, from the Peierls substitution (4.4). Schrödinger electrons give equally spaced levels \(E_n=\hbar\omega_c(n+\tfrac12)\), equation (4.6), while Dirac electrons give \(E_n\propto\sqrt{nB}\), equation (4.7), and this explains the much higher temperature scale of the quantum Hall effect in graphene.

  • In the symmetric gauge, the wavefunctions of the lowest Landau level are holomorphic, \(\psi_m(z)\propto z^m e^{-|z|^2/4\ell_B^2}\), equation (4.10). The many-body state of the filled LLL is the Vandermonde determinant times a collective Gaussian, equation (4.11).

  • When we raise the Vandermonde factor to an odd power \(m\) we obtain the Laughlin wavefunction, equation (4.12), with filling factor \(\nu=1/m\), equation (4.13). Its quasihole excitations, equation (4.14), have fractional charge \(e/m\), equation (4.15), and follow anyonic statistics. They get a phase \(2\pi/m\) for a full braid, equation (4.16), which is an exchange phase \(\pi/m\) strictly between the bosonic \(0\) and the fermionic \(\pi\), and this is the simplest case of fractionalization that we will meet.

  • The Haldane model shows that the quantized Hall conductance never strictly needed a magnetic field, and that it is adiabatically and topologically equivalent to the quantum Hall state driven by the field. This is a return to session 3 and not new theory.

In this session the order was driven only by a magnetic field, which is an external symmetry-breaking parameter that we switched on by hand. In the next session we will see a completely different route to order, which is the attractive interaction between the electrons themselves. This interaction drives the system to become a superconductor without any external field, and we will see that the same classification into trivial and chiral topological states, which we built here and in session 3, comes back to sort superconducting order parameters into conventional and topological classes. It is also useful to keep in mind the form of fractionalization that we just built, since we will soon meet a second example of it, which looks quite different. Here, the correlations inside a partially filled topological flat band split the charge of the electron and made its exchange statistics anyonic. In the next session, pairing correlations in a superconductor will split the electron into two Majorana halves instead. The underlying idea is the same, which is a correlated topological state with excitations that are not the electron itself, but it is reached by two really different mechanisms.
