% Session 4 -- lowest-Landau-level orbitals as nested rings, and the uniform
% droplet they add up to at nu=1 and at nu=1/3.
% Placement: sec. "Angular momentum, enclosed flux, and the uniform electron gas".
%
% Panel (a) plots |psi_m|^2 = r^{2m} e^{-r^2/2} (l_B = 1), each renormalized to unit
% peak so the ring positions can be compared; the peak is at r_m = sqrt(2m).
% Panel (b) is an explicitly SCHEMATIC flat-top profile (a logistic edge), not a
% literal sum of Gaussians: what matters is that the total is flat out to
% sqrt(2(N-1)) l_B and then drops, and that at nu = 1/3 the same N electrons cover
% sqrt(3) times the radius at one third the density.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s04:droplet}{Angular momentum fixes the radius. (a) The lowest-Landau-level
orbitals (4.10), $|\psi_m|^2\propto r^{2m}e^{-r^2/2\ell_B^2}$, each renormalized here to the same
peak height. They are nested rings, and when $m$ increases the ring moves outward, to
$r_m=\sqrt{2m}\,\ell_B$, and encloses more flux. (b) When we add every orbital from $m=0$ to
$m=N-1$ we therefore fill a disc, and the rings overlap in such a way that the total density is
flat inside the disc and falls off sharply at the edge. The filled lowest Landau level is a
uniform electron droplet without structure, with one electron per flux quantum. The Laughlin state
(4.12) at $\nu=1/3$ is the second profile. The same $N$ electrons now use orbitals up to
$m=3(N-1)$, so the radius of the droplet grows by $\sqrt{3}$ and the density becomes a third,
while the droplet stays as uniform as before. The Laughlin construction is built to keep this
uniformity, and the edge profiles are schematic.}
