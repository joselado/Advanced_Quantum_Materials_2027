% Session 4 -- Peierls phases on a square lattice in two different gauges, and the
% gauge-invariant plaquette sum.
% Placement: sec. "Choosing a gauge: Landau versus symmetric", after the two bullets.
% Rendered by figures/blender/s04f02_gauge.py, labels in figures/overlays/s04f02_gauge.tex.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s04:gauge}{Two gauges for the same magnetic field, written as Peierls phases
(4.4) on a square lattice, with a dial on every bond whose needle points at the angle of its phase,
drawn for $\varphi=\pi/4$. (a) In the Landau gauge the horizontal bonds have no phase at all and the
vertical bond in column $x$ has the phase $x\varphi$. The phase pattern depends on $x$ but not on
$y$, so translations along $y$ are still an exact symmetry and a band structure in $k_y$ still
exists. (b) In the symmetric gauge every bond has a phase and none of them is the same as in (a), so
no translation survives, but the pattern is invariant under rotation about the centre of the
plaquette. There is no physical difference between the two. When we go once around any plaquette,
such as the shaded one, the phases of the single bonds add up to the same $\varphi$, which is the
flux through that plaquette. The bond phases are a choice of gauge and the loop sum is not, and the
electrons respond only to the loop sum.}

\end{aqmfigure}
