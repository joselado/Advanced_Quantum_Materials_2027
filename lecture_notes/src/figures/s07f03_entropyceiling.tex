\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s07:ceiling}{The bond dimension is an upper limit on the entanglement, and
this determines when matrix product states work. An MPS with bond dimension $M$ has at most $M$
Schmidt terms across any bond, so by (7.10) it can have at most $S=\log M$ there, which is shown by
the two horizontal bars, for $M=4$ and $M=16$. A gapped one-dimensional ground state follows the
area law of Section 7.6, and its entropy saturates at a value set by the correlation length and not
by the subsystem size, so it stays below a fixed and moderate limit for any chain length. A critical
chain violates the area law logarithmically, (7.12), so it goes above any fixed limit at some point,
here the lower bar, and $M$ must be increased as the system grows, slowly but without bound. A
general state of the Hilbert space of dimension $2^L$ follows a volume law, $S=\ell\log 2$, and it
goes above any usable $M$ after a few sites. This is why time evolution over long times, highly
excited states and two-dimensional systems are all out of reach. The entropy is drawn in bits,
$S/\log 2$, with central charge $c=1$, and the curves are schematic and not fits to a specific
model.}
