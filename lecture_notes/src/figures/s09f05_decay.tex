% Session 9 -- the session's general diagnostic: power-law decay means gapless,
% exponential decay means gapped.
% Placement: sec. "Impurities as diagnostics IV", after equation (9.11).
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s09:decay}{The diagnostic that connects the whole session, drawn for the two
Heisenberg chains of (9.11). Close to the impurity both look the same, with an induced moment that
changes sign from site to site, because the exchange is antiferromagnetic. The only difference
between them is how the envelope decays. (a) The $S=1/2$ chain maps, through the Abrikosov-fermion
construction of session 8, onto a \emph{gapless} spinon band with a real Fermi surface of two points,
so its envelope decays as the power law $1/n$. This is the same mathematics, and the same reason, as
for the Friedel oscillations (9.9) of a metal. (b) The $S=1$ chain has the Haldane gap, which fixes a
finite correlation length $\xi$ and gives an exponential envelope $e^{-n/\xi}$, in the same way as
charge screening decays inside a band insulator. (c) On a logarithmic vertical scale the two are
easy to distinguish, since an exponential is a straight line of slope $-1/\xi$ and a power law is
not. The statement can be read in both directions, so a measured decay law is a measurement of
whether the ground state is gapped.}
