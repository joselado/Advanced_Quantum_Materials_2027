\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s09:plasmon}{The two divergences of the RPA response (9.6), drawn in the
$(q,\omega)$ plane for the three-dimensional electron gas. The shaded region is where
$\mathrm{Im}\,\chi_0(q,\omega)\neq0$, which is the \emph{particle-hole continuum}, the set of all
$(q,\omega)$ at which a single electron can be moved from an occupied state $k$ to an empty state
$k+q$. Its upper boundary is $\hbar\omega_+=\hbar v_Fq+\hbar^2q^2/2m$, and beyond $2k_F$ a lower
boundary $\hbar\omega_-=\hbar^2q^2/2m-\hbar v_Fq$ appears. Both follow from the energy denominator
of (9.5), and for $q<2k_F$ the continuum goes all the way down to $\omega=0$, which is the same
statement as saying that $\chi_0$ is built only from states on the two sides of the Fermi surface.
The plasmon (9.7) is a different object. It is a zero of the RPA \emph{denominator} $1-v(q)\chi_0$,
so it exists at $\omega_p$ even as $q\to0$, in a window where no single-particle excitation exists
at all. This window, marked in the figure, is the full meaning of the phrase ``collective mode, not
a particle-hole pair''. Once the branch meets the continuum it can decay into particle-hole pairs,
and it is Landau damped (dashed).}
