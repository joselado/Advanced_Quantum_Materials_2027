\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s07:tensors}{The graphical notation, and what the MPS ansatz looks like in it.
(a) A tensor is a dot and each of its indices is a leg, so a scalar is a bare point, a vector has one
leg, a matrix two, and so on; joining two legs means summing over that shared index. (b) The exact
coefficient tensor of (7.4) is a single object with one leg per site, and it hides $2^L$ independent
numbers. The matrix product state (7.5) replaces it by a chain of small tensors, each carrying one
physical leg pointing down and virtual legs of dimension $M$ linking it to its neighbours, for
$\sim 2LM^2$ numbers in total: the exponential wall is traded for a chain that grows linearly. (c) An
operator, the Hamiltonian above all, becomes a matrix product operator with two physical legs per
site. Applying it to a state means gluing the diagrams along the shared physical legs and contracting
them away, which multiplies the two bond dimensions together; a singular value decomposition keeping
only the largest singular values then truncates the result back to a workable $M$. That truncation is
the engine inside every MPS algorithm, DMRG included.}
