% Session 9 -- three regimes of translational symmetry, three kinds of eigenstate,
% and the inverse participation ratio that separates them.
% Placement: sec. "Disorder and Anderson localization", after equation (9.12).
%
% Panels (b) and (c) are exact eigenstates nearest the band centre of an N = 89
% site chain, diagonalized numerically: (b) the Aubry-Andre-Harper potential at its
% self-dual point V_0 = 2t with q = (sqrt(5)-1)/2, (c) uniform random on-site
% disorder of strength W = 4t. Panel (d) averages the IPR over the states nearest
% the band centre at each N.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s09:localization}{The three regimes of Section 9.10, shown through their effect
on an eigenstate of a chain of $N=89$ sites. (a) With a periodic potential every eigenstate is a
Bloch wave, with weight exactly $1/N$ on every site, and this height becomes smaller as the chain
grows. (b) At the self-dual point of the quasiperiodic model the state is neither extended nor
localized. It has structure on every length scale at the same time, with large peaks and smaller
copies of the same pattern between them, and this state is called critical, or multifractal. (c) With
really random disorder the state is concentrated on a few sites and it is strictly zero, to numerical
precision, over most of the chain. \emph{Note the three different vertical scales.} For this reason
we need a number and not a visual check, and this number is the inverse participation ratio (9.12).
(d) The IPR separates the three regimes clearly by how it scales with system size, with
$\mathrm{IPR}\sim1/N$ for extended states, $\mathrm{IPR}\sim O(1)$ for localized ones, and an
anomalous power $N^{-\tau}$ with $0<\tau<1$ for the critical state between them. On a log-log plot
these are three straight lines with three different slopes.}
