\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s06:frustration}{How the geometry decides if an antiferromagnet can order. (a)
On a bipartite lattice the sites can be coloured with two colours, here dark and light, every bond
joins the two colours, and the N\'eel state of Section 6.10 satisfies all bonds at the same time.
(b) On a triangle it is not possible to use two colours. If we fix two spins antiparallel, the third
one is parallel to one of its neighbours for any choice, the two ghost arrows, and one bond stays
frustrated. For classical spins, this collinear compromise costs $+JS^2$ on the frustrated bond, and
the total is $-JS^2$ per triangle. (c) The energy is lower if no single bond is fully satisfied.
Three spins at $120^\circ$ in the plane give $\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j=-S^2/2$ on \emph{every}
bond and a total of $-\tfrac32 JS^2$, which is lower than (b) by half of its value. This is the
origin of the noncollinear order to which the mean-field solution of the corresponding electronic
Hamiltonian also converges.}
