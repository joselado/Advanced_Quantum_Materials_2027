\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s05:qsh}{How to build the Kitaev chain from parts that exist. The edge of a
quantum spin Hall insulator is helical, which means that on the same edge spin-up electrons move in
one direction and spin-down electrons in the other, drawn in a warm and a cold color. A ferromagnet
placed on a part of this edge breaks time-reversal symmetry locally and removes the Kramers
protection there, which opens a gap in the edge below it, where the channels are drawn faded. With
two ferromagnets, one short helical segment survives between them. This segment has only one
direction of propagation for each spin, so if we count channels it is a \emph{spinless}
one-dimensional wire. This is the ingredient that (5.19) needs, and nature does not give it to us
directly. Coupling it by proximity to an ordinary $s$-wave superconductor gives the pairing, and the
result is a one-dimensional $p$-wave superconductor with a Majorana zero mode at each end of the
segment, which means at the two ferromagnet-superconductor interfaces. Note what was \emph{not}
needed. No unconventional superconductor appears in the recipe, only an $s$-wave one, a magnet and a
topological insulator.}
