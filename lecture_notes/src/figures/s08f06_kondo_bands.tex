% Session 8 -- the Kondo lattice band structure: a flat f level anti-crossing a conduction
% band, and the gap that turns the metal into a Kondo insulator.
% Placement: sec. "Electronic structure of the Kondo lattice: the Kondo insulator", after
% the anti-crossing paragraph.
%
% NOTE (physics): plotted from (8.21) per spin, in one dimension for legibility, with
% epsilon_k = -2t cos k and the f level at zero. The eigenvalues of [[eps_k, -gamma_K],
% [-gamma_K, 0]] are (eps_k +- sqrt(eps_k^2 + 4 gamma_K^2))/2. The filling assumed is the
% one the construction dictates: one f fermion per site from the constraint (8.11), plus a
% half-filled conduction band, so the lower hybridized band is exactly full.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s08:kondobands}{Why a lattice of local moments is an insulator. Left, with the
Kondo coupling switched off, (8.21) is a dispersive conduction band $\varepsilon_{\mathbf k}$
together with a completely flat band of $f$ levels. This band is flat because the auxiliary
fermions have no hopping of their own, and the two bands cross at the Fermi level, which gives a
metal. Right, a finite hybridization $\gamma_K$ removes the crossing. The bands repel each other,
the states close to the crossing become mixtures of conduction electron and local moment, and a gap
opens exactly where the crossing was. With one $f$ fermion per site from the constraint (8.11) and
a half-filled conduction band, the lower hybridized band is exactly full. So the Fermi level is
inside the gap, and the metal has become a \textbf{Kondo insulator}. The gap grows with $\gamma_K$,
and so with the Kondo coupling $J_K$, and the nearly flat band edges that remain are the reason why
the remaining quasiparticles are heavy.}
