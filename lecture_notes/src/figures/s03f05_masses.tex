\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s03:masses}{Two ways to gap the Dirac cones of the honeycomb lattice, on the
lattice and in the spectrum of a zigzag ribbon. Equation (3.8) gives each gapped Dirac point a Chern
number $\tfrac12\,\mathrm{sgn}(m)\,\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)$, and the two Dirac points of the honeycomb
lattice are time-reversal partners with opposite chirality, $\lambda=+1$ at $K$ and $\lambda=-1$ at
$K'$. So the result depends only on how the mass term itself behaves under time reversal. (a) A
staggered sublattice potential, $+m$ on the large dark sites and $-m$ on the small light ones, is an
ordinary scalar potential, which is even under time reversal, so $m$ has the same sign at both
valleys. The two half-integers are then opposite and they cancel, which gives an ordinary insulator
with the same kind of gap as boron nitride, and the ribbon spectrum below it has a gap with nothing
in it. (b) The Haldane coupling, the second-neighbor hoppings drawn as arrows around one hexagon, is
a pattern of circulating orbital currents, which is odd under time reversal, so $m$ changes sign
between the valleys. The two half-integers are then equal and they add, which gives $C=\pm1$. This
is a real Chern insulator with quantized $\sigma_{xy}$ and chiral edge states, built only from
lattice hoppings, with no external magnetic field and zero net flux, and in the ribbon spectrum one
branch from each edge, the dark and the light tube, crosses the gap. The two gaps look the same in
the bulk spectrum, and the difference appears only in the wavefunctions. The spectra are computed
for ribbons 16 cells wide, with $m=0.5$ and $t_2=0.1$ in units of the first-neighbor hopping.}

\end{aqmfigure}
