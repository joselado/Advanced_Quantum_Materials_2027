\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s10:nnqs}{Neural network quantum states. \textbf{(a)} What the ansatz is. The
exponentially long table of coefficients in (10.2) is not compressed, it is never written down at
all. The configuration $s$ is given as an $L$-pixel ``image,'' here a row of up and down spins, and
the network returns the one coefficient $C_\theta(s)$ that we ask for. This is the same structure as
in figure~\ref{fig:s10:template}, where only the input and the output have a new label. \textbf{(b)}
Training. The variational energy (10.3) is the loss, and we estimate it by sampling configurations
from the Born distribution and averaging the local energy (10.4), the bars and their mean. This is
what makes the method possible, since $E_{\rm loc}(s)$ only needs the few matrix elements $H_{ss'}$
that a sparse Hamiltonian connects to $s$, and never the full $2^L\times2^L$ matrix. \textbf{(c)}
The nested regions drawn on the slides. A matrix product state is a highly structured special case
of a PEPS, a PEPS is in turn a state that a large enough NNQS can represent, and NNQS is the outer
region because it imposes the least structure of all on how $C$ depends on $s$. This picture
organizes the lecture and is not a theorem, since the outer inclusion is a statement about networks
that are expressive enough and not about every architecture. Note also that expressiveness has a
cost, because the order is the opposite one for how easy each family is to optimize.}
