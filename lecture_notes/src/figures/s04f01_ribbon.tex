% Session 4 -- the ribbon spectrum: flat bulk Landau levels plus chiral edge
% branches, and why an impurity cannot backscatter an edge electron.
% Placement: sec. "Why an insulating bulk still conducts: edge states", after the
% ribbon / no-backscattering paragraph.
%
% LAYOUT NOTE: everything inside the axis is placed in "axis cs:"; the real-space

% off an anchor node that itself lives in axis description cs.
%
% PHYSICS NOTE: the chirality is fixed by the text's own guiding centre
% x_0 = +hbar k_y / B (sec. 4.9). k_y > 0 puts the guiding centre on the RIGHT
% half of the ribbon, so it is the k_y > 0 branches that ride up the right wall;
% there dE/dk_y > 0, hence v_y > 0: the right edge carries +y movers. The left
% edge is the mirror image. The overall sense follows the chapter's +e carrier
% convention of (4.3); for electrons every arrow here reverses at once, which is
% why this circulation is opposite to the one drawn in fig:s03:chiraledge.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s04:ribbon}{The quantum Hall ribbon. (a) The spectrum of a strip that is
infinite along $y$ and finite along $x$. In the bulk the Landau levels (4.6) are exactly flat, so
they carry no current at all, but near each boundary the guiding centre $x_0=\hbar k_y/B$ is
pushed into the wall and the level bends up strongly. These bending branches are the edge states,
and each of them crosses the chemical potential exactly once per edge, so the number of chiral
modes at a given edge is equal to the number $N$ of filled Landau levels, here $N=2$. (b) The same
statement in real space. The branches with $k_y>0$ have $v_y=\hbar^{-1}\partial_{k_y}E>0$ and live
on the right edge, and the branches with $k_y<0$ move in the opposite direction and live on the
left edge. To reverse the velocity of an electron we would therefore have to move it to the other
side of the sample, which no local impurity can do, and this is why the edge transport has no
dissipation.}
