\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s05:gapsymmetry}{The sign structure of $\Delta(\mathbf k)$ over the Brillouin
zone, ordered by angular momentum $L$ as in (5.14). The positive regions are raised and the negative
ones are sunk, in two colors, and the nodes are where the two colors meet. A reflection of a panel
through its centre sends $\mathbf k\to-\mathbf k$. Panels with even $L$ do not change and are
therefore spin singlets, and panels with odd $L$ come back with every sign flipped and are spin
triplets, which is (5.12) seen in a picture. Counting lobes is the fastest way to identify the
order. Two lobes mean $L=1$, four mean $L=2$ and six mean $L=3$, and any pattern whose lobes
alternate an odd number of times in a half turn is necessarily odd in $\mathbf k$, so it is a
triplet. This is why the $f$-wave state (e), which has six lobes on the hexagonal zone of the
triangular lattice, is a spin triplet, even though it is similar to the singlet $d$-wave state in
every other respect. Extended $s$-wave (b) deserves a second look, since its node is radial and not
angular. It has the same even parity and the same point-group symmetry as plain $s$-wave, and only
the position of its node ring relative to the Fermi surface decides whether it is nodal in the
spectrum. The overall sign of $\Delta$ is a gauge choice, and only the sign \emph{changes} are
physical.}
