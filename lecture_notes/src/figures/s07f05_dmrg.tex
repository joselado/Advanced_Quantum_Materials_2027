\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s07:dmrg}{How DMRG turns an exponentially large minimization into a sequence
of small ones. (a) The numerator of the Rayleigh quotient (7.11) is a single closed network, made of
the ket MPS, the Hamiltonian as an MPO and the bra MPS, all contracted together. It has no free
legs, so it is a number. (b) Every tensor enters that network once in the bra layer and once in the
ket layer, so the energy is a quadratic form in each tensor and linear in its bra copy, and the
derivative with respect to the bra tensor at site $k$ removes it, which is the ghost. What is left
has exactly three open legs, drawn in red, the two virtual bonds and the physical index of site $k$,
which are the indices of the tensor $M_k^{s_k}$ that we want to find. Everything to the left and
right of $k$ has been reduced to an environment, and the stationarity condition is a generalized
eigenvalue problem, which is an ordinary one in an orthonormal gauge, with the size of a single
tensor. (c) We solve it, move one site along, solve again, and sweep back and forth until the energy
stops decreasing. This is the density matrix renormalization group, and at no point do we build an
object of size $2^L$.}
