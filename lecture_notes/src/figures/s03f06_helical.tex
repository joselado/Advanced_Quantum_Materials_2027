\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s03:helical}{Schematic edge dispersions for the two topological classes
of Section 3.8. The grey blocks are the bulk bands, and the branches that cross the bulk gap are the
edge states, plotted as a function of the momentum along the edge. (a) A Chern insulator has a single
branch per edge. At any energy in the gap there is one state and it moves in one direction, so a
scatterer has no state to send it back into. (b) A quantum spin Hall insulator has two branches, one
for each spin, which propagate in opposite directions on the \emph{same} edge, and this is what
\textbf{helical} means. The total Chern number vanishes, but the spin Chern number does not (3.9).
(c) The crossing is not protected because the two spin sectors are separately conserved. It is
protected by Kramers' theorem, since the crossing is at a time-reversal-invariant momentum, where a
twofold degeneracy is guaranteed for spin-$1/2$ electrons for any perturbation that respects time
reversal. Disorder that preserves time reversal can therefore deform the branches but cannot gap
them. Only a perturbation that breaks time reversal opens the gap $\Delta$ in (c), and then the
protection is lost.}
