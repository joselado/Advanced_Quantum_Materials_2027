\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s03:chiraledge}{Chiral edge states, and why they cannot be backscattered, seen
from above with $B$ out of the page. (a) A quantum Hall sample. In the bulk the electrons move on
closed cyclotron orbits and do not go anywhere, and the system is an insulator with a gap between
Landau levels. At the boundary these orbits cannot close. They skip along the edge, and for a given
direction of $B$ they skip in one direction only, which is opposite on the two edges. Propagation in
a single direction is what the word \textbf{chiral} means, and it carries the current that
$\sigma_{xy}=C\,e^2/h$ predicts, even though the bulk is gapped. (b) The consequence. An impurity at
the edge can deflect the mode, but there is no state that propagates in the opposite direction at
the same edge and the same energy for it to scatter \emph{into}, so the mode goes around the
obstacle and continues. Without a backscattering channel there is no resistance, which is why the
Hall plateaus are quantized so sharply. If we reverse the sign of $B$, all the arrows are reversed
at the same time.}
