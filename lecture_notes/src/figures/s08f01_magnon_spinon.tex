\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s08:magnonspinon}{Why the two kinds of magnet have excitations whose spin
differs by exactly a factor of two. (a) In a symmetry-broken magnet every site has a definite spin,
shown with arrows. Flipping one costs $\Delta S=1$, and this excitation is the magnon of session 6.
The single flipped spin drawn here, next to the ghost of its old direction, only does the counting
of $\Delta S$, since the real magnon is the delocalized precession of figure~\ref{fig:s06:magnon}
and not one flipped site. (b) In a quantum magnet no site has a definite spin at all. The sites are
paired into singlets (8.3), drawn as capsules, so $\langle\mathbf S_i\rangle=0$ everywhere and
$S_\text{tot}=0$. When we break one pair, we leave \emph{unpaired} sites, drawn as open balls, and
each one of them has $S=1/2$. This is a spinon, which is half a magnon. For this reason spinons are
always created in pairs, and figure~\ref{fig:s08:vbsrvb} shows whether the pair stays bound or
separates.}
