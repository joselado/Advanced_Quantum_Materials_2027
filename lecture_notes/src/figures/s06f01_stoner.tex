\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s06:stoner}{A graphical reading of the Stoner criterion (6.3). (a) The
self-consistency condition $m=f(m)$, where $f$ is the right-hand side of the zero-temperature gap
equation. It is an odd function that saturates once the bands are fully polarized, and its slope at
the origin is exactly $U D(E_F)$. If this slope is below one, the only crossing with $y=m$ is the
trivial solution $m=0$. Once the slope is above one, a pair of nontrivial solutions $\pm m_0$
appears and the nonmagnetic state becomes unstable. (b) The resulting order parameter, which stays at
zero until $U D(E_F)$ reaches one and then grows continuously. For this reason, whether a metal
becomes magnetic is not decided by $U$ alone but by the \emph{product} of $U$ with the density of
states at the Fermi level. This is why aluminium and lead stay nonmagnetic even with a sizeable
Coulomb repulsion, while the narrow $d$ bands of iron and cobalt are clearly above the threshold.}
