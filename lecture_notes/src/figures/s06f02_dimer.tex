\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s06:dimer}{Superexchange in the two-site Hubbard model, and the reason why
it is always antiferromagnetic. (a) With parallel spins, the only possible hop would put two
$\uparrow$ electrons on one site, and Pauli exclusion forbids this completely. As a result the
triplet states cannot lower their energy at any order, and stay at exactly $E_T=0$. With
antiparallel spins the hop is allowed, so the singlet goes through a virtual double occupation of
energy $U$ and comes back, and gains a second-order energy $-4t^2/U$. Here the magnetic order is a
purely kinetic effect, since antiparallel spins have lower energy because only they can move. (b) The exact singlet energy $E_S=\tfrac{U}{2}-\sqrt{(U/2)^2+4t^2}$ as a function of
$U/t$, with $E_T=0$ exact for every $U$. The singlet-triplet splitting \emph{is} the Heisenberg
coupling $J$ of (6.4), and it decays as $4t^2/U$. This is the same $1/U$ law that we found
numerically for the mean-field energy difference between the ferromagnet and the antiferromagnet in
Section 6.5.}
