\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s02:flatbands}{Flat bands that come only from geometry, with the bands
computed from the Bloch Hamiltonian of each lattice and the flat band drawn as the red tube.
\textbf{(a)} The Lieb lattice, which is a square lattice with one site in four removed, and
\textbf{(b)} its bands. There are two dispersive bands and a band that is exactly flat at
$\epsilon=0$, and all three touch at $M$. \textbf{(c)} The kagome lattice, made of triangles that
share corners, and \textbf{(d)} its bands, with a Dirac-like crossing at $K$ and a flat band at
$\epsilon=2t$. \textbf{(e)} The reason why the flat band exists. An eigenstate can be confined to a
single kagome hexagon, with amplitudes of alternating sign around it, the filled and the
ghost balls. Every path by which it could hop onto a site outside arrives twice with opposite sign, the
two arrows, so the two contributions cancel at the cross. Nothing is fine-tuned to zero, since the
flatness is a property of the geometry of the lattice, and by (2.16) it makes the density of states
diverge.}
