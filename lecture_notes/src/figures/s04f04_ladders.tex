% Session 4 -- Landau level ladders: Schroedinger (equally spaced, linear in B)
% against Dirac (sqrt(n) spacing, sqrt(B) scale, with an exact zero mode).
% Placement: sec. "Landau levels of Dirac electrons", after equation (4.7).
%
% Level positions: (a) E_n = (n+1/2) hbar omega_c, n = 0..5, drawn with the band
% bottom as the baseline. (b) E_n = sgn(n) v_F sqrt(2 hbar B |n|), n = 0,+-1,+-2,+-3,
% drawn as 2.6 + 1.3*sgn(n)*sqrt(|n|) so that |n|=4 would land on the panel edges.
% The two vertical scales are independent -- only the PATTERN is being compared,
% which is what the caption says.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s04:ladders}{The same magnetic field with two dispersions gives two completely
different ladders. (a) Schr\"odinger electrons give the harmonic-oscillator spectrum (4.6), where
every level is a constant $\hbar\omega_c=\hbar B/m$ above the one below it, and the whole ladder
grows linearly in $B$. (b) Dirac electrons give the square root of that spectrum, equation (4.7).
The levels come closer together as $\sqrt{n}$, they come in a particle-hole symmetric pair for
every $|n|\ge1$, and the overall scale grows only as $\sqrt{B}$. This is still enough to put the
level spacing of graphene about two orders of magnitude above the one of a GaAs quantum well at
the same field. The $n=0$ level is the qualitatively new feature. It stays at exactly zero energy
for every field strength, because it is the only solution with no partner in the other spinor
component. The two vertical scales are independent of each other, since what we compare is the
pattern of spacings and not the absolute energies.}
