% Session 4 -- the Laughlin quasihole: a depletion of fractional charge, and the
% Berry phase that measures both its charge and its exchange statistics.
% Placement: sec. "Berry phase, fractional charge, and anyonic statistics",
% after equation (4.16).
%
% NOTE ON PHASES: (4.16) is the phase for a FULL LOOP of one quasihole around the
% other. A full loop is two exchanges, so the exchange phase -- the number that is
% 0 for bosons and pi for fermions -- is pi/m. The interpolation strip at the
% bottom is drawn in exchange phase, and is labelled as such. The winding sense is
% left unlabelled on purpose: its sign depends on the field/charge convention.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s04:quasihole}{Fractional charge and fractional statistics, both read off a
Berry phase. (a) Multiplying the Laughlin state by $\prod_i(z_i-w)$, equation (4.14), raises every
electron's angular momentum about $w$ by one, which by figure~\ref{fig:s04:droplet} pushes charge
radially outward and leaves a depleted spot behind. Doing it $m$ times removes exactly one
electron's worth of charge, so one quasihole is short of an electron by a factor $m$: it carries
$q=e/m$. (b) Carrying one quasihole once around another and tracking the Berry phase separates
that statement into two pieces: an Aharonov-Bohm piece, proportional to the enclosed flux, whose
prefactor is what fixes $q=e/m$ in (4.15), and a leftover piece $2\pi/m$ that depends on nothing
but the fact that one quasiparticle went around the other. A full loop is two exchanges, so the
exchange phase itself is $\pi/m$, drawn at the bottom: for every odd $m>1$ it lands strictly
between the bosonic $0$ and the fermionic $\pi$, which is what makes these excitations anyons.}
