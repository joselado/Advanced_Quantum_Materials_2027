% Session 8 -- the emergent gauge structure: link phases are gauge-dependent, plaquette
% fluxes are not, and a single flipped flux is a vison.
% Placement: sec. "Emergent gauge symmetry and vison excitations", after the square-lattice
% example.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s08:gauge}{The emergent gauge theory of the parton construction, and its
excitation. (a) The mean-field bond variable lives on the links,
$\chi_{ij}=|\chi_{ij}|e^{i\theta_{ij}}$, and the local phase rotation (8.16) shifts every link
phase by $\theta_i-\theta_j$. Going once around a plaquette, each site phase is picked up twice
with opposite signs, so the flux $\Phi=\prod e^{i\theta_{ij}}$ is untouched: the link variables are
pure gauge, the flux is physical. This is exactly why two mean-field solutions related by (8.16)
describe the same spin liquid. (b) The flux is therefore what labels the state. With $\Phi=+1$ on
every plaquette as the ground state, flipping a single plaquette to $\Phi=-1$ costs energy and
cannot be undone by any gauge transformation: that localized defect of the gauge field is a
\textbf{vison}. Which link carries the minus sign is a gauge choice and can be moved around at
will; the shaded plaquette cannot.}
