% Session 6 -- ferromagnetic versus antiferromagnetic magnon dispersions, eqs. (6.8) and (6.9).
% Placement: sec. "Magnon dispersions: ferromagnets versus antiferromagnets", after eq. (6.9)
% and the "quadratic in ferromagnets, linear in antiferromagnets" paragraph.
%
% Drawn for the one-dimensional chain, z = 2, gamma(k) = cos(ka), so that
%   E_FM  / (|J| S z) = 1 - cos(ka)        and      E_AFM / (J S z) = |sin(ka)|.
% Both curves are therefore plotted in the same units, |J| S z.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s06:dispersion}{Magnon dispersions (6.8) and (6.9) for a chain
($z=2$, $\gamma(\mathbf k)=\cos ka$), both in units of $|J|Sz$. Both vanish at $\mathbf k=0$: a
uniform rotation of every spin costs nothing, so the magnon is the Goldstone mode of the broken
spin-rotation symmetry and no amount of exchange can gap it. What differs is \emph{how} they
vanish. The ferromagnet's $1-\gamma(\mathbf k)$ is quadratic in $k$; the antiferromagnet's
$\sqrt{1-\gamma(\mathbf k)^2}$ is the square root of the same quantity and so is linear, a
Dirac-like cone rather than a parabola. The right panel magnifies the zone centre, where the
difference is unmistakable. That extra square root is precisely the fingerprint of the Bogoliubov
transformation the N\'eel state forced on us: the anomalous $a_ib_j$ terms are what turn a parabola
into a cone.}
