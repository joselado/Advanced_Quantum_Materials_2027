% Session 6 -- what a magnon is in real space, and the Holstein-Primakoff bookkeeping.
% Placement: sec. "The Holstein-Primakoff transformation", at the paragraph that states the
% bookkeeping (S^- creates a magnon, S^+ destroys one) after eq. (6.7).
%
% The side view and the top view use the same phase, 45 degrees per site: the arrow tip in
% the side view sits at (0.32 cos(ka n), 0.15 sin(ka n)) relative to the top of the cone,
% which is the foreshortened projection of the in-plane arrow drawn in the top view.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s06:magnon}{What (6.6)--(6.7) actually describe. (a) The ferromagnetic ground
state: every spin at its maximal $S^z=S$, and therefore zero magnons. (b) A single magnon is
\emph{not} one spin flipped over on one site. It is every spin tipped slightly out of alignment and
precessing on its cone, with the precession phase advancing by $\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\delta$
from site to site (seen most clearly in the top view, where the transverse component turns steadily
and closes on itself after one wavelength). The missing unit of spin is therefore shared coherently
across the whole lattice: $\sum_i\langle S_i^z\rangle=NS-1$. Because the tipping angle is small,
$\langle a_i^\dagger a_i\rangle\ll S$ everywhere, which is exactly the condition under which the
square roots in (6.6) may be expanded; the tipping angle is drawn far larger here than it
really is. (c) The counting rule that makes the transformation easy to
remember: the boson number counts spin removed from saturation, so the \emph{lowering} operator
$S^-$ creates a magnon and the \emph{raising} operator $S^+$ destroys one.}
