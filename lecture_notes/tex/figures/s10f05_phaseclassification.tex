% Session 10 -- phase classification: where a local order parameter works and where it fails.
% Placement: sec. "Phase classification", after the Ising-gauge-theory paragraph.

% NUMERICAL PROVENANCE (all configurations below were generated and checked in a
% throwaway script before being hard-coded here; nothing is eyeballed):
%   (a) 6x6 Ising sites, 4 spins flipped  -> m = +0.78
%   (b) 6x6 Ising sites, 19 up / 17 down  -> m = +0.06  (no fully polarized row or column)
%   (c) 5x5 vertex grid, spins on the 40 BONDS, built as sigma_(vw) = eps_v eps_w with
%       random eps_v = +-1 on the vertices.  Every plaquette product is then automatically
%       +1 (each eps appears twice around any plaquette); verified explicitly: 0 of the 16
%       plaquettes violated.  Bond magnetization m = +0.05.
%   (d) same 40 bonds drawn independently at random: 7 of the 16 plaquettes have product
%       -1 (marked), and m = -0.05, i.e. indistinguishable from (c) by magnetization alone.
% The star flip highlighted in (c) is the gauge transformation: the four bonds meeting at a
% vertex each belong to exactly two of the four adjacent plaquettes, so flipping all four
% leaves every plaquette product +1 (checked in the same script).
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s10:phaseclass}{Why one phase-classification problem is easy and the other is
hard. \textbf{(a,b)} For the Ising model (10.9) the magnetization of a single snapshot already
separates the phases, and a network trained on such snapshots is only rediscovering the
local-order-parameter logic of sessions 1, 5 and 6. \textbf{(c,d)} For the Ising gauge theory
(10.10) the spins live on the \emph{bonds}, and the $T=0$ condition $\prod_{i\in\partial p}\sigma_i
= +1$ leaves an exponentially large degenerate manifold: a typical ground state is bond-by-bond
random, so the magnetization is $\approx 0$ in both columns and no single-bond quantity tells them
apart. What differs is the four-spin plaquette structure, marked in the plaquette centres, and
finding a function of the raw configuration sensitive to it is what the convolutional network has
to do for itself. This is the same obstruction that forced sessions 3 and 4 from local order
parameters to Chern numbers and Zak phases.}

% ---- verdict, row 2 ---------------------------------------------------------
\node[anchor=north west, text width=6.3cm, align=left, font=\small] at (8.45,-2.35)
 {\textbf{Local order parameter fails.}\\[3pt]
  Flipping the four bonds that meet at a single vertex changes two bonds of each
  neighbouring plaquette, so every plaquette product survives: the $T=0$ manifold is
  $2^{N_{\rm v}}$-fold degenerate and a typical member of it is bond-by-bond random.
  Summing the spins therefore returns $\approx 0$ on both sides, and the classifier has to
  key on the plaquette correlations instead.};
