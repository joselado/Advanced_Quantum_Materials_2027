% Session 8 -- geometric frustration: why triangles, and why kagome.
% Placement: sec. "Frustration: the route to a quantum spin liquid", after the
% triangular-plaquette paragraph.
%
% NOTE (physics): the spins are drawn as Ising (collinear) configurations, exactly as
% the text's argument is phrased. For the Ising antiferromagnet on a triangle the ground
% manifold is 6-fold degenerate (three inequivalent placements of the unsatisfied bond,
% times a global flip); panel (c) shows the three inequivalent ones. The macroscopic
% degeneracy comes from tiling these triangles corner-to-corner, panel (d).
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s08:frustration}{Frustration, with the spins drawn as Ising (collinear)
variables throughout, which is how the argument in the text is phrased; allowing them to rotate
freely instead resolves the triangle into the $120^\circ$ noncollinear state of
figure~\ref{fig:s06:frustration}, and the degeneracy discussed here is the Ising one.
(a) On a bipartite plaquette every
antiferromagnetic bond can be satisfied at once ($\checkmark$), and the ground state is the
N\'eel state: two configurations, related by a global flip, each breaking time-reversal symmetry.
(b) On a triangle it cannot be done: once two spins are antiparallel the third bond is stuck
($\times$), whichever way the last spin points. (c) The frustrated bond can sit on any of the three
edges, so the classical ground state is degenerate rather than unique; quantum fluctuations mix the
degenerate configurations into a single superposition with $\langle\mathbf S_i\rangle=\mathbf 0$,
which does \emph{not} break time-reversal symmetry. (d) The kagome lattice of herbertsmithite is
built entirely from corner-sharing triangles, so every one of them is frustrated at once and the
classical degeneracy becomes macroscopic: this is why triangular and kagome lattices are the
hunting ground for quantum spin liquids.}
