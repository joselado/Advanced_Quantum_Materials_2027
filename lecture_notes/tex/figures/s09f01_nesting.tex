\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s09:nesting}{Nesting is what makes a response function diverge.
(a) A one-dimensional band $\varepsilon(k)=-2t\cos k$ at partial filling: the occupied states (thick)
end at $\pm k_F$, and the single wavevector $q^\star=2k_F$ connects one Fermi point to the other, so
the energy denominator of (9.4) vanishes for that term. (b) The resulting static Lindhard function
(the closed form quoted in Section 9.6): finite and equal to $D(E_F)$ as $q\to0$, exactly as the
$q\to0$ check of (9.4) requires, and logarithmically divergent at $q^\star=2k_F$ --- the Peierls
instability. (c) In two dimensions the criterion becomes geometric. At half filling the square
lattice's Fermi surface is the straight-edged diamond $|k_x|+|k_y|=\pi$, and because
$\varepsilon(k+Q)=-\varepsilon(k)$ for $Q=(\pi,\pi)$, that one vector maps \emph{every} point of one
edge onto the parallel opposite edge simultaneously. This is why the divergence at $(\pi,\pi)$ is so
much stronger than the one-dimensional one: whole segments of Fermi surface nest, not isolated
points. At low filling the same Fermi surface is a small circle, which no single $q$ can nest this
way.}
