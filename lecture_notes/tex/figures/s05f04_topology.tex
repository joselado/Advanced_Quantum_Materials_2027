% Session 5 -- same gap, different topology: trivial s-wave versus chiral p-wave.
% Placement: sec. "Same gap, different topology", after the bulk-boundary paragraph.
%
% The dials are drawn IN THE COMPLEX PLANE but PLACED at the momentum where Delta is
% evaluated; this is the one thing the figure must not let the reader confuse, so each dial
% is given its own little frame and the panel labels say it in words. On a small circular
% pocket, sin k_x + i sin k_y is approximately k_F e^{i phi}: constant modulus, phase equal
% to the momentum angle. That is exactly why the two states are spectrally alike and
% topologically different.
%
% The lower row is deliberately a REAL-SPACE cartoon and not a ribbon band structure: the
% ribbon spectrum is what the notebook asks the reader to compute.
\begin{aqmfigure}{fig:s05:topology}{Two gaps that a spectroscopy experiment cannot tell apart. Each
dial is the phase of $\Delta(\mathbf k)$, an arrow in the complex plane, drawn at the point of the
Fermi pocket where it is evaluated. (a) For $s$-wave pairing that phase is the same everywhere, so
as $\mathbf k$ goes once around the pocket the value of $\Delta$ never moves off a single point of
the complex plane: winding number zero, Chern number zero. (b) For chiral $p$-wave pairing (5.15)
the phase instead tracks the direction of $\mathbf k$, so $\Delta$ traces a loop that encircles the
origin once. The modulus is essentially the same in both cases, so both fully gap the same pocket
by the same amount; only the winding differs, and the winding is what the Chern number counts.
(c) The consequence on a finite sample: the trivial state is gapped at its boundary as well as in
its bulk, while the chiral state must carry $|C|$ gapless branches on each edge, all running the
same way, and the opposite way on the opposite edge. Here $C=\pm2$, one branch from each of the two
identical spin blocks of (5.8).}
